# A1 – Create Portfolio

## Objective
The objective of this assignment is to create a professional engineering
portfolio that documents my ability to analyze engineering problems, make
and justify design decisions, and communicate my reasoning so that another
person can understand and reproduce my work.

## Analyze
### Task A: Portfolio Analysis

#### Portfolio 1: Matthew Smith – Mechanical Engineering Portfolio

For my first portfolio, I looked at Matthew Smith's Mechanical Engineering
Portfolio. The website is organized so that his different projects can be
found directly from the main page. Each project is separated and labeled
based on what he worked on, such as his mechanical ping-pong ball server,
four-bar linkage calculator, and topology-optimized beam. Because the
projects are separated and easy to identify, I would expect a visitor
looking for a specific project to be able to find it in under 60 seconds.

In terms of reproducibility, the portfolio explains what Smith worked on
and gives the reader an understanding of the engineering involved, but I
do not think I could completely reproduce each project using only the
information provided. More information about dimensions, materials,
calculations, and the exact procedures used would be needed.

The portfolio also shows evidence of engineering reasoning because the
projects include information about the engineering methods and skills used
to reach the final designs instead of only showing finished products. The
language is appropriate for an engineering audience because the
descriptions focus on technical work, objectives, and results.

Overall, the main strength of this portfolio is that an employer can
quickly identify the projects Smith completed and the engineering skills
demonstrated by them. Its main limitation is that another engineer would
need additional technical documentation to reproduce the projects.

#### Portfolio 2: Jason Taylor – Mechanical Engineering Design Portfolio

For my second portfolio, I looked at Jason Taylor's Mechanical Engineering
Design Portfolio on GitHub. His portfolio contains several engineering
projects, including a syringe plunger stopper pin, vacuum pick-and-place
station, fill-volume verification post, master's thesis, and femoral
fracture analysis. The projects have individual headings and descriptions,
which makes it possible for a reader to locate a particular project
without searching through the entire repository.

Taylor's portfolio also provides evidence of the reasoning behind some of
his engineering decisions. For example, in the vacuum pick-and-place
project, he explains that oval suction cups were used to increase the
available suction force while working within the limited space around the
part. This gives the reader a reason for the component choice instead of
only showing the finished design.

However, I would still need additional dimensions, calculations,
specifications, and procedures to completely reproduce the system. His
descriptions use engineering terminology such as tolerance stack-up,
positional repeatability, fluid-flow modeling, and parametric studies.
This communicates the projects in terms that an engineering reader can
interpret.

Overall, Taylor's portfolio provides useful evidence of why certain
engineering decisions were made, but additional quantitative
documentation would make the projects easier for another engineer to
reproduce.

### Comparison

After comparing the two portfolios, I would want my own portfolio to
combine parts of both approaches. I would use Smith's organization to
make individual assignments easy for a visitor to find while also
including the engineering reasoning shown in Taylor's project
descriptions. I would include equations, assumptions, calculations,
design choices, and results so another engineering student or engineer
could understand what I did, why I did it, and how I reached my final
result.
